Solar Battery Gateways Pathway · EU Compliance

Compliance is the sales instrument, not the cost line.

Four EU instruments land between September 2026 and 2028, and together they change what a manufacturer is allowed to sell into Europe. None of them can be satisfied by a certificate at the border. Each one asks for data that had to be originated at the device and cannot be reconstructed afterwards. That is a problem for every manufacturer without silicon inside the product, and an advantage for the first ones that have it.

The clock, in order of arrival

11 SEP 2026
CRA Article 14 reporting
Manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to ENISA. In force since 11 September 2026, including for products already on the market.
18 FEB 2027
EU Battery Passport
Mandatory for EV, LMT and industrial batteries above 2 kWh. No passport, no placing on the market.
18 AUG 2027
Battery due diligence
Supply chain due diligence obligations, deferred from 2025 by Reg. (EU) 2025/1561.
11 DEC 2027

Essential cybersecurity requirements and conformity assessment for all products with digital elements.
1 JAN 2028
CBAM downstream
Scope set to widen. Three lists tabled, none of them law, and none containing CN 8541.

    EU-01

    NIS2, or why your customer’s customer is asking

    Read this if a European integrator has suddenly sent you a security questionnaire.

    The NIS2 Directive treats electricity as an essential sector. Utilities, DSOs and large integrators inside the EU are legally obliged to manage cybersecurity risk across their supply chain, which they discharge by pushing requirements down their contracts to component suppliers. Most manufacturers outside Europe meet NIS2 not as a filing obligation of their own, but as a procurement wall: the questionnaire that arrives before the purchase order, and the clause that follows it.

    Incident reporting runs on a tight clock, with an early warning due within 24 hours of a significant incident. A supplier who cannot support that timeline is a supplier the integrator has to explain to its regulator.

    Directive (EU) 2022/2555 · eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj

    EU-02

    Cyber Resilience Act, the one that binds the manufacturer directly

    Read this if you ship any connected product: inverter, gateway, BMS, monitoring board.

    NIS2 lands on the operator. The CRA lands on you. Any product with digital elements placed on the EU market carries obligations on the manufacturer: secure by default configuration, vulnerability handling for the support period, a software bill of materials, and CE marking backed by conformity assessment.

    Two dates matter. 11 September 2026, when Article 14 reporting begins: actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents must be notified to ENISA and the relevant CSIRT, with an early warning inside 24 hours. 11 December 2027, when the full essential requirements and conformity assessment apply.

    Reporting runs through ENISA’s Single Reporting Platform, live the same day. And read Article 69(3) before you assume this is a problem for future products only: the reporting obligation extends to every in-scope product already on the EU market. Hardware you shipped years ago is inside the 24 hour window the moment an exploitation is found.

    The practical consequence for a hardware maker is that a coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy and a named security contact stop being good practice and become an entry condition.

    Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 · eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj

    EU-03

    Network Code on Cybersecurity, where inverter fleets get caught

    Read this if your devices are aggregated: DER portfolios, VPPs, fleet managed inverters.

    Beyond the horizontal rules there is a sector specific network code for the cybersecurity of cross border electricity flows. It matters to hardware makers because it is the instrument that reaches aggregated fleets, thousands of small devices under one control channel, rather than only large plant. If your gateway or inverter can be commanded remotely at scale, it sits inside a risk perimeter someone is now formally required to assess.

    Read the timing carefully. The 4 hour incident clock in Art. 38(3) is real, but it binds only high and critical impact entities, and binding identification of those entities is not due until 13 June 2028. Anyone demanding a 4 hour supplier clock from you today is running ahead of the instrument.

    Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1366 · eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2024/1366/oj

    The industry’s own position

    The one document to read if this looks like a European bureaucrat’s idea.

    This is not regulators pushing against industry. In July 2024 SolarPower Europe published its own position calling for a harmonised cybersecurity baseline for solar PV: stronger governance under NIS2, product security through the CRA, a dedicated standard for distributed energy resources, operational PV data held within the EU or equivalent security jurisdictions, and an EU level monitoring layer for manufacturer coordinated devices such as inverters.

    Read it as a forecast. Where the trade body asks for a baseline, a baseline arrives.

    EU-05

    Battery Passport, the hard gate at 18 February 2027

    Read this first if you assemble battery or BESS packs.

    From 18 February 2027, every EV, LMT and industrial battery above 2 kWh placed on the EU market must carry a digital battery passport, reachable by a QR code on the battery itself. The passport holds a unique identifier, model and technical characteristics, carbon footprint specific to the manufacturing site and batch, and performance and durability data, with tiered access for the public, regulators and end of life processors.

    The carbon footprint declaration applies 18 months after the later of the delegated act and the implementing act it depends on enters into force, and the Commission has not yet adopted them. Due diligence obligations follow on 18 August 2027, deferred from 2025 by Regulation (EU) 2025/1561.

    The commercial point is not the passport. It is that batch level carbon and lifecycle data has to exist before the pack leaves the line. Reconstructing it from ERP records afterwards is what auditors are built to find.

    One battery obligation is already live. Since 18 August 2024, Article 14 requires the battery management system of a stationary storage system to hold up to date state of health and expected lifetime data, readable by the buyer and by anyone acting for them. A buyer’s insurer or lender can only rely on that data if it cannot be edited after the fact.

    That machinery is no longer battery only in its reach. The Global Battery Alliance and the Solar Stewardship Initiative signed a memorandum of understanding on 22 September 2026 to align traceability, assurance and sustainability reporting across both sectors, which puts passport thinking in front of module makers well before ESPR does. See EU-07.

    Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 · eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj

    EU-06

    ESPR, the passport model spreading beyond batteries

    Read this if you make modules and think batteries are somebody else’s problem.

    The Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation is the framework that generalises the battery passport into a Digital Product Passport for other product groups, set by delegated acts over the coming years. It is the instrument to watch for photovoltaics: it carries product level carbon, durability, repairability and recycled content requirements, and it amends the Battery Regulation directly.

    Nothing binds solar modules under ESPR today. The exposure is that the mechanism now exists and has been proven on batteries first.

    Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 · eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/oj

    EU-07

    Solar Stewardship Initiative, the audit regime buyers already use

    Read this if you sell modules into European utility scale tenders.

    Ahead of any binding regulation, European developers, buyers and financiers have converged on the Solar Stewardship Initiative’s ESG and Supply Chain Traceability Standards as the evidence they will accept. Traceability here means documented chain of custody from polysilicon through wafer, cell and module, the thing most supply chains cannot demonstrate because the record was never bound to the physical unit.

    This one is voluntary, and it is the one already appearing in tender conditions. It is where a manufacturer can differentiate this year rather than in 2028.

    On 22 September 2026 the SSI and the Global Battery Alliance signed a memorandum of understanding in Brussels covering supply chain traceability, sustainability assurance, stakeholder engagement and capacity building. The work named in it is joint guidance, webinars and workshops, working group exchanges, and pilot initiatives on responsible sourcing, traceability and sustainability reporting. The GBA brings the practical experience it built on the Battery Passport to the solar side of the table. The first public output is a joint webinar with the Global Solar Council on 21 October 2026, on battery passports for solar and energy storage.

    SSI’s own dashboard, as of 16 October 2026: 79 assessments, 23 certified sites, 13 of them certified to both standards, and no certified wafer, metallurgical silicon or quartz site yet. Against that, manufacturer members have committed that at least 80% of the modules they ship to the EEA, UK and Switzerland come from sites certified to both standards by 1 January 2027, and all of them by 1 January 2028. The gap is upstream evidence, the hardest record to reconstruct afterwards.

    Read that as direction of travel. The solar and the battery traceability regimes are converging on one evidence model. A manufacturer building a separate audit trail for each is building the wrong thing twice. The position that gets cheaper over time is one attested record per physical unit that both regimes can read.

    solarstewardshipinitiative.org, Supply Chain Traceability Standard · SSI and GBA partnership, 22 September 2026

    EU-08

    CBAM, stated precisely

    Read this before anyone tells you the carbon border tax applies to your panels.

    CBAM entered its definitive phase on 1 January 2026. Its scope is cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. Solar modules and batteries are not in scope.

    Your live exposure today is the aluminium frame (CN 7604, 7606, 7610) and the steel mounting structure (CN 7308, 7318). Those are CBAM goods now. The module itself, CN 8541 43 00, is not.

    From 1 January 2028 the scope is set to widen, and three lists have been tabled: the Commission’s of December 2025 (about 180 CN codes), the Council’s general approach of 12 June 2026, and the European Parliament environment committee’s of 6 July 2026 (277 further codes). Between them they reach photovoltaic generators (CN 8501 71 00, 8501 72 00, 8501 80 00), inverters above 7.5 kVA (CN 8504 40 86) and heat pumps. None of the three lists contains CN 8541. None of them is law yet, and the final list will be settled in a Parliament and Council negotiation not expected to conclude before the end of 2026.

    So the risk to watch is a classification risk rather than a policy one. A product built and declared as a photovoltaic generator lands inside the committee’s list; the same silicon sold as a module does not. If you integrate, get a Binding Tariff Information ruling rather than assuming.

    taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu, CBAM

    EU-09

    GBA Battery Passport, the certificate on top of the legal one

    Read this if a European buyer asks which sustainability scheme your packs are in.

    The EU passport is a legal record. The Global Battery Alliance is building the voluntary certificate that sits on top of it: a product level score for each battery, built from facility level reports across the supply chain against 18 benchmark modules, independently verified, with the scheme launching in early 2027. Its 2026 Operational Trials ran with 17 supply chain partnerships whose cell makers account for 80% of global production, among them CATL, CALB, HiTHIUM, Jinko ESS, REPT BATTERO, Sunwoda, EVE Energy and Gotion.

    The assurance chain is ISO from end to end. The GBA acts as scheme owner under ISO 17067, verifiers are accredited to ISO 17029, certifiers to ISO 17065, and the mark on the physical battery follows ISO 17030. Verification is risk sampled, so a facility is not audited every year, and the record between audits has to hold on its own.

    The trials produced two lessons that matter to a pack maker: supply chain data exchange does not require unrestricted data sharing, and independent verification is what makes the result count with buyers. HiTHIUM, reporting from inside the trials at the GBA, SSI and Global Solar Council webinar of 21 October 2026, added three more: link traceability to physical material lots, tier disclosure so confidential data stays protected, and do not expect a grace period for missing data once the QR code is printed.

    All five describe the record, not the report. A unit identity bound at manufacture, signed data a verifier can check without seeing a competitor’s inputs, and the verifier’s attestation written to the same record. Build it once and it serves the legal passport, the GBA certificate and, on the solar side, the SSI audit.

    GBA, Battery Benchmarks, Beta Release, September 2025 · globalbattery.org, 2026 Battery Passport pilots · GBA, SSI and GSC webinar, 21 October 2026

    Physical AI, part 1: the dark factory and shipping logistics

    Four sections on the factory and the road out of it: how unit identity is bound today, what UNITY adds at each station, what reads the unit in transit, and why a printed code is not enough. Each section stands alone.

    PAI-01

    Lights-out lines still bind their data through a database row

    Read this if your plant is automated and you assume traceability is solved.

    The public marker for factory automation in this sector is the World Economic Forum lighthouse list. The first lighthouse in each segment: CATL Ningde for batteries (2021), LONGi Jiaxing for modules (December 2023), Tongwei Meishan for cells (2025) and HiTHIUM Chongqing for energy storage batteries (January 2026). LONGi Jiaxing alone runs 784 cameras across 20 processes.

    The identity model underneath has not changed. On a module line, EL runs before lamination, and the flash test (IV curve, Pmax) runs after framing and junction box. On a cell line, a laser marks a Data Matrix or QR code on the can at grading, after formation. In both cases the MES links each measurement to the printed code. The binding lives in a row in the maker’s database, and the unit carries only a symbol anyone can copy.

    No lighthouse disclosure or MES vendor material we reviewed signs unit measurements with a key held in the unit. The secure elements sold for batteries answer one question, whether a battery is original. On the sources we read, none of them signs a measurement.

    PAI-03

    Shipping and warehouse: what reads the unit, and what the passport asks for

    For logistics, goods-in and anyone building the passport.

    Inside a warehouse, fixed portals and forklift-mounted readers read pallets in bulk, with forklift readers reaching up to 12 m. Outside, drone readers are commercial for outdoor yards, typically reading 2 to 8 m below the aircraft on flights of 20 to 40 minutes. One reader vendor advises drones for yards and forklift readers for indoor continuous inventory.

    The EU battery passport applies from 18 February 2027 to industrial batteries above 2 kWh, EV and LMT batteries, and the passport is reached through a QR code linked to a unique identifier (Art. 77(3)). EN 18220:2026 on data carriers, published as a harmonised standard by Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1736 (in force 15 July 2026), accepts QR Code, Data Matrix, NFC, HF RFID and RAIN UHF RFID as data carriers.

    No published passport text requires the carrier to be signed. The companion standard on data authentication and integrity, EN 18246, was still under approval in mid 2026. A carrier tells a reader where the record is. A carrier does not prove that the pack in hand is the pack the record describes.

    UNITY puts Bluetooth plus RFID on the unit, so one signed identity is read at the portal, by the forklift, by the drone over the yard and by a phone at goods-in. The QR code stays. UNITY adds the proof behind it.

    PAI-04

    A QR code is not good enough

    Read this when someone says the label already does the job.

    A QR code points to a record. Copy the label, and the copy points to the same record.

    An RFID tag laminated in the glass is a QR code you cannot see. India has required one inside the module laminate for MNRE scheme modules since 1 April 2013, and the current PM Surya Ghar guidelines still ask for “a RF identification tag laminated inside the glass”. The tag holds the factory record: maker, dates, origin, I-V data and serial. The tag is written once, no text we found requires a signature, and it has no sensor. Anyone with a writer can write plausible values.

    The cost of a label that is not bound to the measurement is visible in the data. Fraunhofer ISE CalLab found that in 2023 crystalline modules measured on average 2.2% below their nameplate, against under 1% in 2012 to 2016. Modules are sorted into 5 W classes, and about 2% is enough to move a module up a class. In a 2016 case, a distributor ordered 250 W modules and received 240 W modules carrying replacement 250 W nameplates.

    UNITY signs the flash result to the unit’s own key at the flasher. A relabel or a bin shift then shows up at goods-in, read through the box.

    PAI-06

    Field maintenance: faults found by position, not by proven serial

    For O&M providers, warranty teams and insurers.

    Cleaning robots and inspection drones are commercial. TrinaTracker launched Aurora, an unattended cleaning robot, alongside Buildex in September 2026. Drone thermography locates a fault “down to the module level”, which means by position in the array. No maintenance robot or drone source we found reads the module’s identity in the field.

    Warranty evidence rests on imagery standards: capture at 3 cm per pixel, and repeated inspections to show when a defect began and whether the cause is manufacture or installation. None of that proves the imaged module is the serial named on the claim.

    With UNITY, the module answers a Bluetooth query with its signed identity and its own signed measurements. The drone or the robot binds the observation to the unit at the moment of capture, and the warranty claim carries the panel’s own record. A BESS rack answers the same way.

    PAI-07

    Retirement and disassembly: the passport asks for data per unit

    For recyclers, second-life operators and pack designers.

    The Battery Regulation already sets the data a recycler and a second-life buyer receive:

    • Annex XIII, dismantling information per battery model: exploded diagrams, disassembly sequences, the type and number of fastening techniques to unlock, and the tools required.
    • Annex XIII, per individual battery: state of health, and status as original, repurposed, re-used, remanufactured or waste.
    • Art. 14: since 18 August 2024, read-only access to state of health in the BMS for the purchaser and independent operators.
    • Art. 77(7): a repurposed battery gets a new passport linked to the passport of the original.

    Machine-readable dismantling data per unit matters because robots still struggle to know what they face. The RAPID platform (University of Colorado, March 2026) removed top-cover fasteners from full-scale EV packs with 97% success using taught poses, and 57% using one-shot vision.

    Photovoltaic end-of-life is still keyed to a printed serial label, where the label is legible. The voluntary PV passport recommendation from the European Solar PV Industry Alliance names a QR code as the carrier.

    A pack or panel that announces its own signed identity tells the disassembly cell which dismantling record to fetch before the first cut. The same key carries the signed use history to the second-life buyer and links the new passport to the original.
